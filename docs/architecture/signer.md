# Signer

What `naru-signer` is: the one process that holds the keys of naru's authorities and issues certificates, so that no other process, orch included, can make an identity. It is the first of garamsh/aurora#811's A2 to A5 built; the roles, the revocation list, the admission statement and the encrypted files it uses are `lib/identity`'s ([README](../../lib/identity/README.md)).

## Current decisions

- **`naru-signer` is a binary of the `host/` module,** with its code in `host/internal/signer` and its composition in `host/cmd/naru-signer`. It imports `lib/identity` and no other unit. The image carries it as `/bin/naru-signer` ([ADR 0050](./adr/0050-the-image-carries-orch-and-the-signer-and-init-runs-them-as-separate-users-from-spec-orch-spec-signer-and-spec-metrics.md)).
- **Its directory holds every key and is private.** `--dir` (default `/var/lib/naru/signer`) must be mode 0700 and owned by the user the signer runs as, or it refuses to start. Each file is 0600; a key file with a wider mode, or a directory with some of the authorities and not all, is refused at start.
- **What `init` makes.** The host, operator and orch authorities (ECDSA P-256, ten years), the revocation key (Ed25519), the first root certificate for a certificate request the caller supplies, and the empty revocation list at sequence 1. It records the recovery key's public half and writes the revocation key's public half as a PKIX PEM (`--revocation-public-key`), which orch verifies the list with. A directory that holds authorities is not initialised again. No person makes, places or rotates one of these authority keys, and no `naructl` command takes one ([ADR 0121](./adr/0121-the-signer-makes-and-holds-its-authorities-signs-each-again-under-its-key-two-years-before-it-ends-and-an-enrollment-key-enrolls-one-host-within-a-day-superseding-adr-0040-in-part.md) item 1).
- **An authority is signed again under its key two years before it ends** (ADR 0121 item 3). A serving signer checks at start and every 24 hours, and signs again each authority with less than `identity.AuthorityRenewYears` (2) of its `identity.AuthorityYears` (10) left: same key and subject, a new serial, valid ten years from then, written in one commit and logged at warning level. `GET /v1/ca`, the bundle and a replica carry the new certificate from then on. A certificate the old one signed verifies against the new, and one the new signed against the old while the old lasts. Each signer checks on its own, so two members may serve different certificates of one authority, each valid. Every pool that trusts an authority takes the new certificate from `GET /v1/ca` within those two years, the bound by which it must refresh; orch warns of a pool within a year of its end ([orch.md](./orch.md) §Events). Rotation to a new key, with a cross-signed overlap, is not built (garamsh/naru#451).
- **Every certificate an authority signs is signed through `crypto.Signer`** (ADR 0121 item 2): the key in the directory now, a KMS or HSM key behind the same interface later, which no caller sees. The revocation key signs the list through `lib/identity`'s `SignCRL` with its Ed25519 key, and moves behind `crypto.Signer` in garamsh/naru#412 (e).
- **Roles and who may do what.**

- **Where it listens.** HTTP on a unix socket (`--socket`, mode `--socket-mode`, default 0660); the group that may reach it is the deployment's. `GET /v1/crl` (the signed revocation list) and `GET /v1/ca` (the three authority certificates and the revocation key's public half) answer anyone who reaches the socket. `GET /v1/bundle` carries its signed request in the header `X-Naru-Request`, base64. With `--peer-listen`, a TLS 1.3 listener serves `POST /v1/replicate` and nothing else. On a Node the socket is also reached through the Node API: `/v1/signer/{path}` with any method, for an `naru:admin` client, is forwarded to `/v1/{path}` of the socket, adds no authority, and leaves the signature of each request as the only authentication ([ADR 0057](./adr/0057-an-operator-reaches-the-signer-through-the-node-apis-admin-role-which-forwards-v1-signer-to-the-signers-unix-socket-and-adds-no-authority.md)).
- **Answers to a refusal.** A path the signer does not serve is 404, and `POST /v1/caller`, which root's issuance of a caller answered before ADR 0116, is 410 naming the paths that replaced it. A caller the authorities do not vouch for is 403, each cause named in `statusOf`: a role that does not allow the call, an unknown role, a revoked or unverifiable certificate, a bad statement or signature, a replay, a wrong passphrase. A malformed request is 400 and a state the signer is not in 409. Any other error is the signer's own failure and is 500; it is not answered 403 as a refusal.
- **Authentication is by signature.** A request is the signed envelope of `lib/identity` (`SignRequest`, `VerifyRequest`): action, expiry, body, the caller's certificate. The signer checks the chain against the operator authority for client authentication, the revocation list, the signature, the action and the expiry, then the role. `identity.Authenticate` and `VerifyRequest` are the authenticator seam: authorisation reads only the `Identity` they return.
- **Host certificates.** `POST /v1/host` takes an admission statement and the host's certificate request. The signer runs `VerifyAdmission` itself, requires the SHA-256 of the request's public key to equal the statement's `hostKeySHA256`, and issues CN=hostID, OU host, the DNS name hostID taken from the statement (never from the request, and no IP address, since a DHCP address can change while the certificate lives, [ADR 0061](./adr/0061-a-host-certificate-names-the-host-id-and-orchs-host-client-accepts-the-address-or-the-host-id.md)), server and client authentication, one year, with the issue logged (host ID, serial, key hash, the operator's name and serial). A host ID is a name: letters, digits, `.`, `_`, `-`, at most 128.
- **Other certificates** carry one year. An orch certificate also carries the DNS names and IP addresses of the certificate request, which root authorised by signing the request. Operator, reader and automation certificates carry client authentication only; host and orch carry both. Root is made only by `init`.
- **Caller certificates** ([ADR 0116](./adr/0116-a-caller-registers-once-with-a-one-time-token-root-asks-the-signer-for-and-a-key-it-makes-where-it-runs-and-renews-its-own-certificate-superseding-adr-0108-item-4s-issuance.md); the certificate is [ADR 0108](./adr/0108-orch-serves-a-versioned-machine-api-that-names-machines-and-never-hosts-to-callers-holding-a-signer-issued-caller-certificate.md) item 4's). A caller certificate has OrganizationalUnit `caller` and the CN the caller's name, a DNS label never taken from the certificate request. It carries client authentication only, under the operator authority, and is valid 90 days. Its serial is recorded as role caller, so it is revocable like any other. `lib/identity`'s `CallerName` reads the name back from a caller's identity.
  - **The token.** `POST /v1/caller-token` (root; body the name and `ttlSeconds`, one hour when 0, at most 24 hours, else 400) answers a registration token once: `naru-ct-`, then the claims (the name, a random nonce, the expiry) and the operator authority's signature over a domain string and the claims, base64url. Any replica takes a token any signer made, and the signer keeps no copy. The log names the token's nonce, never the token.
  - **Registration.** `POST /v1/caller-register` (body `token` and `csr`, no envelope) issues the certificate for the request's key, named by the token. A token that is malformed, not the operator authority's, changed, expired or spent is 403, one answer for all. The token's nonce is signed into the revocation list, until the token's expiry, before the request is read, so a presentation spends a valid token whatever follows (a bad request is 400 and the token is gone). A peered signer that has not taken a list within 30 seconds answers 409 and keeps the token, as for `reissue-root`, before it reads the token, so the 409 says nothing of the token. The list carries the nonce to the other signers.
  - **Finding a token spent twice.** The signer logs `registered a caller` with `token_nonce` and `serial`, and `refused a caller registration` with the `token_nonce` the token claims; orch's member logs its own refusals and limits with `source` and `token_nonce` ([machines.md](./machines.md)). The same `token_nonce` in two signers' `registered a caller` lines is a token spent twice within the sync interval: revoke the name and register again with a new token.
  - **Renewal.** `POST /v1/caller-renew` (signed by the caller's current certificate; body the certificate request of its new key) issues a further certificate for the current one's name. Before the last 30 days of the current certificate it is 409. A revoked or expired certificate, or one of a caller revoked by name, is 403, as for any signed request. A request for the current certificate's key is 400. A certificate renews once: the renewal signs `renew:<serial>` into the list as a nonce until the certificate expires, and a second renewal is 403, here and on every signer the list reaches. The current certificate is not revoked, so it lasts at most 30 days beside its successor; a caller that lost the answer registers again with a new token.
  - **Revoking a caller by name.** `POST /v1/revoke-caller` (root or an operator; body the name, a DNS label, else 400) sets the list's entry for the name to the NotBefore a certificate issued then has, which revokes every caller certificate of the name issued until then, whichever signer issued it, and not a later registration. It also puts on the list every serial the signer recorded under the name that is not on it, in the same signing. The signer records each caller certificate it issues under its name with the serial it renewed from in `callers.json` (0600, optional, carried by the bundle), and the answer is that lineage, each serial with whether the list carries it. A name the signer issued nothing to is revoked all the same.
  - **Root's direct issuance.** `POST /v1/caller-issue` (root only; body the caller's name, optional `days` and the certificate request) is for tests and recovery: valid 90 days by default and at most 90 (more, or fewer than 0, is 400).
- **Revocation.** A revoke adds a serial, signs the list again with the next sequence number and writes it before the signer uses it. A serial already on the list changes nothing. The signer records the role of every serial it issues in `issued.json` of its directory (0600, written before the certificate is handed out, carried by the bundle), and a revoke of a serial recorded as root's needs root. A serial the signer has no record of, one issued before the record existed, is not protected.
- **Rotating a host.** The signer keeps `hosts.json` (0600, optional, carried by the bundle): the serials of each host's certificates by host ID, which `POST /v1/host` records. A rotation is two calls with the host's delivery between them, so the old pair is revoked only after the host holds the new one. `POST /v1/reissue-host` (root or an operator; body the host ID and the new key's certificate request) issues a host certificate for the key (CN and DNS name the host ID, one year) and adds its serial to the host's entry, revoking nothing; a host with no recorded serial is 409. `POST /v1/retire-host` (root or an operator; body the host ID and the serial to keep) signs the list at the next sequence with every other serial of the entry not already revoked and sets the entry to the kept serial, in one commit with `issued.json`; a serial the host does not hold is 409, and a retry with nothing to revoke changes nothing. `POST /v1/register-host` (root or an operator; body the host ID and a serial) records the serial of a certificate another authority issued, such as a `gen node` pair, taking it on the operator's word; a serial recorded as another role's is 400. A replica holds the registry as its bundle carried it. ([ADR 0077](./adr/0077-the-signer-rotates-a-hosts-pair-with-the-old-serial-revoked-registers-offline-pairs-by-serial-and-hostd-refuses-a-revoked-key.md))
- **Revoking a lost host by its ID.** `POST /v1/lookup-host` (body the host ID) answers every serial the signer holds for the host, each with whether the revocation list carries it. `POST /v1/revoke-host` (same body) puts every one that is not on the list on it in one signing and commit, and answers the same listing; the record stays, so a retry changes nothing. A host the signer holds no serial for is 409 naming `naructl signer register-host` ([ADR 0095](./adr/0095-the-signer-lists-and-revokes-every-serial-it-holds-for-a-host-id-so-a-lost-hosts-certificate-is-revoked-without-its-serial.md)).
- **Who reads the list.** Orch's API, orch's host client, `naru-hostd` and the Node API each verify it with the revocation public key and refuse a revoked serial ([ADR 0062](./adr/0062-every-naru-tls-peer-is-checked-against-the-signed-revocation-list-by-one-verifier-in-lib-identity-and-fails-closed-when-the-list-is-older-than-its-max-age.md)). On a Node with `spec.signer`, `naru-hostd` and the Node API read it from the socket's `GET /v1/crl`; a host with no signer reads orch's.
- **Replicas share one list.** `POST /v1/crl` takes a signed list and answers with the list the signer holds afterwards; the revocation key must have signed it, else 403. The list also carries the nonces taken (of reissue requests, caller tokens and caller renewals), each with its expiry, and a nonce past its expiry is dropped at the next signing, and the callers revoked by name, each with its time; of two times for one name the later stands. A list that adds no serial, no live nonce and no caller entry to the signer's own changes nothing. A list with a higher sequence whose serials and live nonces are a superset of the signer's own is adopted and written. Lists that each hold a serial or a live nonce the other lacks are merged and signed again at the higher sequence plus one. A signer that has taken a list this way is peered (an empty `peered` file in its directory) and refuses `reissue-root` until it has taken one within 30 seconds, as it does after a restart until the next push; a signer never pushed to is alone. ([ADR 0052](./adr/0052-a-reissue-nonce-is-signed-into-the-revocation-list-so-orchs-sync-carries-it-to-every-signer-and-a-peered-signer-refuses-a-reissue-until-it-has-taken-the-list-lately.md)) Orch carries the lists between the signers ([orch.md](./orch.md)); each signer's own sequence still rises by its own revokes, so two signers that merge independently sign different lists at the same sequence, and the next sync merges those again until they agree.
- **Bundle and restore.** `GET /v1/bundle` returns every authority key sealed to the recovery key's X25519 public half. `POST /v1/restore` fills a blank directory from a bundle, the path of the recovery key's file and the path of its passphrase file, and root's authorisation signed for the SHA-256 of that bundle; the authorisation is verified against the bundle's own authorities. The certificates issued before the loss verify against the restored authorities. The recovery key is made outside the signer by its holder, is kept offline on USB, and never enters its directory; it is the emergency key, used only for a restore and a reissue of root.
- **Reissuing root.** `POST /v1/reissue-root` is for the holder of the recovery key who has lost root's person file. Its request is signed with the recovery key's Ed25519 half and carries the certificate request of the new root key, an expiry of at most ten minutes ahead and a random nonce; the signer verifies it against the recovery key's public half, refuses a nonce on its revocation list that has not expired, so a captured request is taken once, and refuses with 409 a peered signer (see Replicas share one list) that has not taken a list in the last 30 seconds. It then signs the list once, at the next sequence, with the nonce and its expiry and every serial `issued.json` records as root's, and issues the new root (CN root, OU root, one year) under the operator authority. The signer logs the new serial and key hash, the hash of the recovery key's public half, the nonce and the revoked serials. The revocation and the nonce reach another signer by the list sync, and that signer refuses the old root and the replayed request from then on. A root serial with no record is not revoked by it. ([ADR 0051](./adr/0051-the-recovery-key-signs-a-once-only-request-for-a-new-root-certificate-and-the-signer-revokes-every-root-serial-it-recorded.md), nonce and sync: [ADR 0052](./adr/0052-a-reissue-nonce-is-signed-into-the-revocation-list-so-orchs-sync-carries-it-to-every-signer-and-a-peered-signer-refuses-a-reissue-until-it-has-taken-the-list-lately.md))
- **Replication.** A blank signer's `join-key` makes an ephemeral X25519 key; root signs an authorisation for its host ID and that key; `join` posts it to a running signer's peer listener, pinned to the orch authority, and receives the bundle sealed to the ephemeral key. The peer listener's serving certificate is signed by the orch authority in memory with no key on disk. The new signer verifies root's authorisation against what it received before it writes, and removes the ephemeral key.
- **No authority key leaves the directory unencrypted.** The bundle and the replication answer are sealed; the operator's key is in its encrypted person file; the recovery key is encrypted. A test scans a bootstrap's temporary root for private keys outside the directory.
- **`naructl signer rotate-host|register-host`** sign the calls above the same way. `rotate-host` makes the new key, has the signer reissue, delivers the pair to the host's API with a provisioner's client (`--host-client`, [host.md](./host.md)), checks the serial the host reports with and waits until the host API presents the new certificate, and only then has the signer retire the old serials; the key is written nowhere but the host. `register-host` reads the serial from the certificate `--cert` names. `docs/operate/rotate-a-host.md` is the procedure.
- **`naructl signer lookup-host|revoke-host`** take `--host-id` and sign the two calls above the same way and print each serial with `valid` or `revoked`. `docs/operate/recover-a-member-or-host.md` part 2 is the procedure.
- **`naructl operator add|revoke`** signs a request with the person file given by `--admin` (opened with `--passphrase-file`) and sends it to the socket, or with `--config` and `--endpoint` through that Node's API; `add` takes the certificate request by path and writes the issued certificate.

- **Deployment.** A Node runs it from `spec.signer` ([config.md](./config.md)) and the `naru-orch` system extension that holds the binary ([ADR 0055](./adr/0055-orch-and-the-signer-are-delivered-as-a-system-extension-a-squashfs-image-init-mounts-read-only.md)): init runs `/usr/lib/naru/extensions/naru-orch/bin/naru-signer serve` as user and group 6001 with `--dir=/var/lib/naru/signer` (0700, owned by 6001) and its socket `/run/naru-signer/signer.sock`, whose group is 6001; orch's user is 6002 with 6001 as a supplementary group, so it reaches the socket and cannot read the directory. A `bootstrap` makes init run `naru-signer init` once, while the directory has no `recovery-public.json`. A `replica` makes it run `join-key` at the first start, printing the public join key (base64 of the 32 bytes) on the console, and, once root has authorised the key for the host ID `metadata.name`, `join` against `replica.join.peer` pinned to `replica.join.peerCA`; neither runs again once the directory holds authorities. A signer whose directory holds no authority serves its socket alone, so that `restore` reaches it, and ends once the directory holds them, so init starts it again with every listener: the peer listener signs its serving certificate with an authority the blank signer did not have. ([ADR 0040](./adr/0040-naru-signer-alone-holds-the-authority-keys-in-a-private-directory-and-issues-certificates-over-a-unix-socket-against-signed-requests-and-a-recovery-key-opens-the-bundle-that-restores-them.md) item 9 has the restore fill a blank directory, which a signer that refuses to serve it could not do.) `--metrics-addr` serves `naru_signer_http_requests_total` by listener, method and code. ([ADR 0050](./adr/0050-the-image-carries-orch-and-the-signer-and-init-runs-them-as-separate-users-from-spec-orch-spec-signer-and-spec-metrics.md))
### Making and using identities with naructl

- **`naructl operator request`** writes a new ECDSA key (0600, never printed) and the certificate request for it; `--der` writes the DER that `naru-signer init --root-csr` reads. **`naructl operator person`** joins that key and its issued certificate into the encrypted person file, and removes the plain key.
- **`naructl orch csr`** does the same for orch, with the DNS names and IP addresses its certificate serves; **`naructl orch issue`** has the signer issue orch's certificate with root's person file.
- **`naructl caller token --name <name> --admin <person file> --passphrase-file <file> --out <file> [--ttl <duration>]`** has the signer make a caller's registration token with root's person file (`--socket`, or `--config` and `--endpoint`) and writes it to `--out` only (0600), printing its expiry. Before it sends anything it refuses to write over a file, a name that is not a DNS label, and a `--ttl` outside 1 s to 24 h. The person installing the caller gives it the token once.
- **`naructl caller revoke --name <name> --admin <person file> --passphrase-file <file>`** has the signer revoke the caller by name with root's or an operator's person file (`--socket`, or `--config` and `--endpoint`), and prints the time the entry holds and each serial the signer recorded as `<serial> valid|revoked renewed-from <serial>|-`.
- **`naructl caller credential --name <name> --admin <person file> --passphrase-file <file> --cert-out <file> --key-out <file> [--days <n>]`**, for tests and recovery, makes a key, has the signer issue the caller certificate for it directly with root's person file, and writes the pair (key 0600). Before it sends anything it refuses to write over a file, a name that is not a DNS label, and more than 90 days.
- **`naructl signer ca`** prints the signer's authorities (`GET /v1/ca`, which needs no signature) as PEM, each after a line with the SHA-256 of its DER, through `--socket` or through `--config` and `--endpoint`; `--authority` names one. **`naructl orch ca`** prints the orch authority alone, which orch serves nowhere.
- **`naructl signer authorise-replica`** signs root's authorisation for a host's signer and its join key; **`bundle`** writes the authorities sealed to the recovery key; **`restore`** fills a blank signer from that bundle. These three are root's.
- **`naructl signer reissue-root`** is the recovery key's, for a lost root person file: `--recovery-key` and `--passphrase-file` are local files, the key is opened on that machine and signs the request, and `--csr` (from `naructl operator request`), `--socket` (or `--config` and `--endpoint`) and `--out` name the request, the signer and the certificate written. `naructl operator person` joins the certificate with the request's key.

## Why

- **A separate process with a private directory** is what keeps orch from minting identities: orch asks, and the signer verifies a signature orch cannot make. ([ADR 0040](./adr/0040-naru-signer-alone-holds-the-authority-keys-in-a-private-directory-and-issues-certificates-over-a-unix-socket-against-signed-requests-and-a-recovery-key-opens-the-bundle-that-restores-them.md))
- **The threat model** is orch, and any other user of the machine, reading the keys. That orch's user cannot read the directory is enforced by ownership at deployment (the user `naru-signer`, the directory 0700), and is stated as such; the signer checks the directory's mode and owner and cannot check how orch is run.
- **Accepted risks:** a signed request can be replayed until it expires, except a reissue of root, whose nonce is on the revocation list every signer takes (a replay on a second signer during the sync interval after the first use still succeeds when its caller reaches that signer's socket, because a push is no proof that orch made it); a root serial the signer has no record of is not revoked by a reissue; an operator can revoke any serial the signer has no record of as root's, and the recovery key reissues root; a caller's registration token presented twice is taken by a second signer that the list has not yet reached, within the sync interval after its first use, as a reissue of root is, and so are a caller certificate's second renewal and a renewal after its name was revoked; anyone who reaches the socket may push a list the revocation key signed, which is a replay of an older list at worst and never removes a serial; a host certificate lasts a year with no renewal until the host renews its own (ADR 0121 item 7, garamsh/naru#412 (b)), and has no subject alternative name; the bundle and the recovery key are the one recovery path; the join is TLS with server authentication, and the binding of root's authorisation to the ephemeral key is its client authentication.
- **The operator's key is a file behind `crypto.Signer`,** so a hardware key replaces the file without a change to a caller.
- [ADR 0121](./adr/0121-the-signer-makes-and-holds-its-authorities-signs-each-again-under-its-key-two-years-before-it-ends-and-an-enrollment-key-enrolls-one-host-within-a-day-superseding-adr-0040-in-part.md): why the signer keeps its keys, as kubeadm does, with no KMS; why it signs again under the same key rather than rotating to a new one; why every pool refreshes within the two years left.
- [ADR 0114](./adr/0114-naru-runs-no-kubernetes-or-gaon-and-their-layouts-programs-images-tests-and-documents-are-removed.md): why the signer issues no exposer certificate.

## Open questions

- **aurora#811 A4 is not built:** `naructl login` with a second factor and short-lived certificates, re-confirmation of dangerous actions, passkeys, hardware keys and single sign-on.
- **Renewal of a host certificate** is decided (ADR 0121 item 7) and not built: hostd renews its own under its key in garamsh/naru#412 (b), orch its own in (c), and root and the operators theirs in (d).
