# Orch

What `naru-orchd` is: the service of the control hosts that keeps the inventory of hosts (enrolled, admitted, in a region, with labels), elects the one member that acts, and answers the API through which operators and hosts reach it. The authorities' keys are not here: they stay with the signer.

## Current decisions

- **`naru-orchd` is a unit.** It is the module `orch/`, with one binary, `cmd/naru-orchd`, which is the composition site. Its types and Go client are the library module `lib/orchapi`, used by naructl and naru-hostd; the roles, the pairing code, the admission statement, the revocation list and the sealed box are `lib/identity`'s ([ADR 0041](./adr/0041-orch-is-a-cluster-of-members-with-an-embedded-etcd-one-elected-leader-and-an-mtls-api-that-enrolls-and-admits-hosts-and-the-signer-holds-every-key.md)).
- **Members.** Each control host runs one member: an embedded etcd v3.6 (peer and client URLs over mutual TLS from the orch certificate, the flags `--name`, `--data-dir`, `--peer-url`, `--client-url`, `--initial-cluster`, `--initial-cluster-state`, `--join`, `--restore`) and the API. With `--metrics-addr` the registry also counts the API's requests in `naru_orch_api_requests_total{route,code}`, by route pattern. Three members tolerate the loss of one.
- **One leader.** The members campaign on an etcd election with a session lease of 10 s. Reconcilers and state derived from the inventory run only while a member leads, with a context that ends when it stops leading and carries the member's term. A stopped leader is replaced within about the lease; a member never resigns early. The groups engine (`orch/internal/groups/`, [groups.md](./groups.md)) and the network reconciler (`orch/internal/netrec/`, §Networks) are the `Reconciler`s orchd runs in that seam, each in its own goroutine. **The term is a fencing token** ([ADR 0071](./adr/0071-an-orch-leader-fences-its-etcd-writes-and-its-host-calls-with-the-revision-of-its-election-key-and-a-host-refuses-a-lower-token.md)): the CreateRevision of the member's election key. Every etcd write the leader makes as leader goes through `leader.Guard`, a transaction that compares that key's CreateRevision to the token, and a failed guard ends the term. Writes of an operator's API request keep the plain client. Every mutating call to a host carries the token in `X-Naru-Leader-Token` ([host.md](./host.md)), and a host's 409 `stale-leader-token` ends the term. A restore adds 1,000,000,000 to the restored store's revision and marks it compacted (`orch/internal/restore`), so the restored cluster's tokens stay above every revision the lost one reached.
- **etcd is bounded and kept small** ([ADR 0088](./adr/0088-orch-compacts-defragments-and-bounds-its-etcd-by-a-share-of-the-data-disk-drops-events-by-age-removes-a-member-that-stays-out-for-it-to-rejoin-empty-and-init-grows-the-data-file-system-to-its-disk.md)). Each member compacts by time (`periodic`, 10 minutes), keeps a backend quota of one eighth of the total size of its data disk within 128 MiB and 2 GiB (`--etcd-quota-bytes` sets it directly; a disk under 1 GiB is refused; `--etcd-compaction-retention` replaces the ten minutes), and keeps two WAL and two snapshot files. The leader's housekeeper (`orch/internal/member`, run every `--housekeeping-interval` (default 1 minute; `spec.orch.housekeepingInterval`) from `cmd/naru-orchd`) defragments one member at a time, only while every member answers and its database is at least 80% of the quota (`DefragHighWater`) with at least 10% of the file free pages (`DefragReclaimable`; [ADR 0104](./adr/0104-the-housekeeper-defragments-a-database-at-eighty-percent-of-its-quota-with-a-tenth-of-the-file-free-in-place-of-an-eighth-of-the-quota-and-half-free-amending-adr-0088-item-4.md), which replaces ADR 0088's eighth of the quota and half free), disarms the no-space alarm of a member under half its quota in every pass (and defragments a member whose alarm only its free pages keep armed), reports a member whose live data is half its quota or more with an error log and an `etcd.quota` event, once per member per term and only while the alarm is not armed, and, after an empty transaction guarded by a compare on its election key, removes the one member that has stayed unreachable for `--depart-after` (default 30 minutes; `spec.orch.departAfter`) when the cluster has three members or more and the others answer. A member that was added and has not started is no member out inside that time, and is removed once it has stayed unstarted for it ([ADR 0092](./adr/0092-the-leader-removes-a-member-that-was-added-and-stays-unstarted-for-the-departure-time-and-does-not-count-it-as-a-member-out-until-then.md)). A member the cluster no longer lists by name records its peers in a marker (`<data-dir>.rejoin`), deletes its member directory at start and joins again through them, whether it started alone, with `--join` or with a static `--initial-cluster` (it asks the peers its own etcd data lists, and acts only on an answer from the cluster its data belongs to); a crash before the join is retried from the marker. An `etcd.quota` event or log error is answered by raising `spec.orch.etcdQuotaBytes` and rebooting the control hosts. While a member's no-space alarm is armed, or its database is over the quota, a write of orch's API is answered `507` with the message `orch's database is full (etcdserver: mvcc: database space exceeded): ...`, which `writeError` in `orch/internal/api` gives for etcd's `rpctypes.ErrNoSpace` wherever a handler's store returns it, and is logged at error level; any other store failure stays a `500 internal error`.
- **The API** is HTTPS, TLS 1.3, port 7500 by default. A client certificate is verified against the authority and read with `lib/identity`; the role is its one OrganizationalUnit. A revoked serial is refused: the revocation list is the signer's, verified with the revocation public key (`--revocation-public-key`), kept 30 s and replaced only by a list whose `seq` is not lower. With no verified list yet every authenticated call is 503; with a list and a signer that stops answering, the last list stays in force for `--revocation-max-age` (default 10 minutes) from its last refresh, and after that every authenticated call is 503 until a refresh succeeds.
- **The Machine API** ([ADR 0108](./adr/0108-orch-serves-a-versioned-machine-api-that-names-machines-and-never-hosts-to-callers-holding-a-signer-issued-caller-certificate.md), [machines.md](./machines.md) §The API). A member started with `--machine-zone` (the zone of the stable names, `spec.orch.machineZone`) serves `machines/v1alpha1` on a listener of its own, `--machine-api-addr` (`0.0.0.0:7600` by default, `lib/wire`'s `MachineAPIPort`; `spec.orch.machineApiListen`); without a zone it serves none and logs so. Every member is started with the same zone: the first to serve records it at `/naru/orch/machine-zone`, and a member started with another answers 503 `unavailable` and logs both zones. The listener takes TLS 1.3 and a client certificate when one is sent, which must verify against the authority; every operation but `register` refuses a request without one (403 `forbidden`). It checks the certificate against the revocation list as the API does, serves the role `caller` alone (any other is 403 `forbidden`), and answers every path outside `/machines/v1alpha1/` with 404 `not-found`; the API on 7500 serves none of its paths. A member that waits for a restore answers it 503 `unavailable`. `orch/internal/machineserver` is the transport and translates every failure to the API's status and code in one place; `orch/internal/machines` keeps the records in etcd, each under its caller, so another caller's is 404:
  - a Machine at `/naru/orch/machines/<caller>/<name>`: the spec as given, env values and file contents included (a read answers neither), the grace of the last stop, the status, and the reconciler's binding (host and epoch), the reason with the fleet's figures and the time of its last restart; its `id`, 16 random lowercase hexadecimal digits, is claimed at `/naru/orch/machine-ids/<id>` in the transaction that creates it. A restore's create is guarded on the snapshot as it was checked, so one deleted meanwhile is not restored;
  - a snapshot at `/naru/orch/snapshots/<caller>/<name>`, recorded `pending` with an id of its own and its Machine's id and spec, in a write guarded on the Machine as read, so one marked deleting meanwhile is not snapshot; the reconciler adds the host it is taken on. Its delete is one transaction that also writes `/naru/orch/snapshot-deletions/<id>` (the host and the disk snapshots' names) when it was taken on a host;
  - each removed Machine's tombstone at `/naru/orch/machine-tombstones/<id>`, written in the transaction that removes it and kept 7 days, and each snapshot deletion kept 7 days once done, as the snapshot's tombstone;
  - each host's allocation at `/naru/orch/host-allocations/<host>`: the CPUs, memory and disk its bindings take, written only by the leader, with the binding or removal that changes it, while it is the one the plan read;
  - each caller's event log under `/naru/orch/machine-events/<caller>/`, apart from the fleet's: an event is appended in the transaction that writes the status it carries, and its cursor is the revision that committed it. The log keeps the caller's newest 20000 events and none older than 7 days; each trim records the revision of the newest event it dropped at `/naru/orch/machine-event-floors/<caller>`, so a cursor below it is 410 `cursor-expired`, and a cursor above the store's revision, or one that is not a revision, is 400.

  A create is recorded `pending`, with the capabilities `snapshot: [volumes]` and `move: [cold]` and the shape's GPUs; start and stop change the desired state as a new generation, a placed Machine showing `starting` or `stopping` at once, and a stop's `graceSeconds` above 240 is 422 `invalid`; a delete marks the Machine `deleting`. Placing, running and removing Machines, and taking snapshots, are the Machine reconciler's: the leader runs it when the binary gives it the hosts (`naru-orchd` does), each host reached as the groups engine reaches it, with `--machine-interval` (default 5 s) between passes and `--host-reserve-cpu` (1) and `--host-reserve-memory` (2 GiB, in bytes) kept for each host ([machines.md](./machines.md) §State, §Placement). Its writes go through `leader.Guard`. Exec and the file calls are answered by the member that took them, which reads the host from the inventory and calls its guest routes without a leader token ([machines.md](./machines.md) §The API); the listener writes an answer for up to 12 minutes, the longest exec and the host's minute to end it, where the API on 7500 keeps 60 seconds. Aliases answer 501 `unimplemented` until they are built. `GET /v1/machines` (reader and up) lists every caller's Machines with their caller, without a secret, a `binding` once one exists, and the `reason` with the fleet's figures while one waits.
  - **A caller's registration and renewal** ([ADR 0116](./adr/0116-a-caller-registers-once-with-a-one-time-token-root-asks-the-signer-for-and-a-key-it-makes-where-it-runs-and-renews-its-own-certificate-superseding-adr-0108-item-4s-issuance.md) item 7). `POST .../register` and `POST .../renew` are passed to the member's own signer (`POST /v1/caller-register`, `POST /v1/caller-renew`, `orch/internal/signer`), which alone checks the token or the caller's signed request; the member adds no authority. Register is the one route of the listener a request without a verified client chain reaches; any other, a path the API does not serve among them, is 403 before its handler. Registrations are bounded in each member's memory (`orch/internal/ratelimit`) per source, an IPv4 address or an IPv6 /64, by a token bucket of their own with enrollment's figures (1 a second, burst 10), and for every source together by one more (1 a second, burst 30). A limiter holds at most 4096 buckets and refuses a new name when none of them has refilled. The member logs a limited registration, and one the signer refused, with `source` and the `token_nonce` the token claims. The signer's refusals keep their meaning (403 `token-refused` or `forbidden`, 409 `not-due`, 400 `invalid`), and any other failure of the signer is 503 `unavailable`.
- **The revocation list is synced between the signers.** Each member stores the highest-sequence list it has seen in etcd at `/naru/orch/crl`, written with a compare-and-swap on the key's revision, and every `--revocation-sync-interval` (default 10 s) pushes it to its local signer with `POST /v1/crl` and stores what the signer returns when its sequence is higher. A lost swap is read again at the next sync. The list also carries the nonces of the signer's reissue requests, which orch stores and pushes as it does the serials ([ADR 0052](./adr/0052-a-reissue-nonce-is-signed-into-the-revocation-list-so-orchs-sync-carries-it-to-every-signer-and-a-peered-signer-refuses-a-reissue-until-it-has-taken-the-list-lately.md)). A revoke on one signer therefore reaches the others within a sync interval plus the member's list TTL; until then a signer that has not heard of it still issues for that operator.

  A `caller` certificate is 403 on every endpoint of this API that takes a certificate. `GET /v1/crl` and enrollment take none, and answer a caller as they answer anyone: the list is public, and enrollment authenticates by key or proof. A caller reaches the Machine API's listener only (below).

- **Enrollment.** An operator creates an enrollment key (`naru-ek-` and 64 hex digits, shown once, valid at most 365 days); orch keeps the SHA-256 of it, never the key. A host sends the key, its ID, its public key and a certificate request to `POST /v1/enroll`; the request is refused with 403 for a key that is unknown, expired or revoked, and with 429 over a token bucket per source address (1 per second, burst 10) and per key (5 per second, burst 50). The buckets are in each member's memory. At most 1000 enrollments wait. The host shows its pairing code on its console every minute and polls `GET /v1/enroll/{hostID}` with a signature of the current time by its own key (five minutes of skew); orch answers a poll only for the holder of the enrolled key: an unknown host ID, a signature that does not verify and one outside the skew all get 404 with one identical body, so a poll does not tell which host IDs exist, and a source over its poll rate (10 per second, burst 100, in each member's memory) gets 429. A pending host with a valid proof gets 200. A request body over its bound is 413. ([ADR 0066](./adr/0066-orch-reserves-a-machine-prefix-before-it-asks-the-signer-and-commits-it-at-admission-keeps-no-credential-in-a-host-record-and-allocates-every-prefix-through-one-function.md))
- **`GET /v1/crl` needs no client certificate.** The list is signed by the revocation key and public; the hosts that are to refuse a revoked peer read it here over `PinCA`, from `spec.host.orch`, and verify it with the revocation public key ([ADR 0062](./adr/0062-every-naru-tls-peer-is-checked-against-the-signed-revocation-list-by-one-verifier-in-lib-identity-and-fails-closed-when-the-list-is-older-than-its-max-age.md)). Orch's host client checks the revocation list on every host certificate through `identity.PeerCheck`, with the same list and max age as the API, so a host whose certificate is revoked is not called. The configuration orch builds for a host that joined from a seed, on each answer to its enrollment poll, carries the key and the signer's current list (`spec.host.revocationPublicKey`, `spec.host.revocationList`); a signer that cannot answer fails the admission and the poll.
- **Admission.** `POST /v1/admit` is for an operator. Orch computes the pairing code from the key it stored and compares it with the request's code; it does not use a code a host sent. A mismatch is 403 and nothing reaches the signer. Otherwise the opaque admission statement and the host's certificate request go to the signer, which verifies the statement (role, revocation, expiry, the key's hash) and issues the host's certificate; orch checks that the certificate is for the enrolled key and stores it. The certificate names the host ID (DNS), and orch's host client accepts a host's certificate that chains to the host authority and names the address it dialled or the host ID, so a host with a DHCP address is reached; `spec.orch.hostCA` holds the authority of the control hosts' API certificates and the signer's host authority ([ADR 0061](./adr/0061-a-host-certificate-names-the-host-id-and-orchs-host-client-accepts-the-address-or-the-host-id.md)). The region and labels are fields of the request beside the statement. naructl's `host admit` takes the key from `GET /v1/pending`, compares the code against that key itself and signs locally with the operator's person file.
- **Hosts.** `GET /v1/hosts` lists each host with its region, address, labels, `lastSeen` and, for a host that joined from a seed, the `machinePrefix` orch allocated (omitted otherwise); `naructl host list` shows them.
- **Region and labels.** A region is a DNS label and may be empty; labels are keys of a name with an optional DNS-subdomain prefix (at most 253 characters) and values (at most 63), at most 64 of them. `PUT /v1/hosts/{id}/labels` replaces both. Every enrollment, admission, refusal of a code and label change is an event, listed by `GET /v1/events`.
- **Events.** The log keeps the newest 20000 events and none older than 7 days ([ADR 0088](./adr/0088-orch-compacts-defragments-and-bounds-its-etcd-by-a-share-of-the-data-disk-drops-events-by-age-removes-a-member-that-stays-out-for-it-to-rejoin-empty-and-init-grows-the-data-file-system-to-its-disk.md)); the ones older than that are dropped by any write that records an event, by the store's clock, and the oldest beyond the count when an event is appended. The leader's housekeeper appends `etcd.quota` (subject a member; detail its database size, live data and quota) when that member's live data is half its quota or more. Each event carries a `cursor`, the etcd revision that committed it, and `GET /v1/events?since=<cursor>` lists only the events after it (`orchapi.Client.EventsSince`); `Events` and a request with no `since` list the whole retained log. The console reads the log by cursor. A cursor that is not a revision is 400.
- **Heartbeat.** An admitted host presents its certificate to `POST /v1/heartbeat`; orch records the time as the host's `lastSeen`. After a restore the host reconnects with the same certificate.
- **Snapshots.** Every member takes an etcd snapshot of its own every 6 hours, keeps the newest 8 and writes each sealed to the recovery public key (`--recovery-public-key`) with `lib/identity`'s sealed box; with no recovery key nothing is written, and an unsealed snapshot is never written. `POST /v1/snapshot` (root) takes one at once. The guest's snapshot directory is `/var/lib/naru/orch/snapshots`; an operator fetches the newest sealed snapshot with `GET /v1/snapshots/latest` (root, the sealed bytes, 404 with none), which `naructl orch backup --out <file>` does after taking one. [ADR 0053](./adr/0053-a-member-started-with-restore-serves-only-a-snapshot-upload-and-the-operator-sends-it-the-plain-snapshot-and-the-other-members-join-it-as-new-members.md)
  - **Restore.** `naructl orch restore` opens a sealed snapshot with the recovery key and its passphrase; `--out` writes the plain etcd snapshot, and `--push <orch URL>` (with `--orch-ca`, `--admin` and `--admin-passphrase-file`) sends it, with its SHA-256 in `X-Naru-Snapshot-Sha256`, to a member started with `--restore`. The plain snapshot is written to the operator's disk only with `--out`.
  - **A member that waits for a restore** has no etcd in its data directory and `--restore` set; it starts no etcd and serves only `PUT /v1/restore`, root only (other routes are 503). It refuses a body whose hash or etcd's own check fails (400), restores into this member's name, `--peer-url` and an initial cluster of itself alone, and starts; a second `PUT` is 409. With a member in the data directory `--restore` is ignored and logged.
  - **Order.** The first member restores alone. The second and third start with `--join <a running member's client URL>` and `--initial-cluster-state existing` (an image writes both from `spec.orch.join` and no `--initial-cluster`; `naructl gen node --orch-join <url>` writes the field), call `MemberAdd` themselves (retried for up to a minute while etcd calls the cluster unhealthy) and take the initial cluster etcd returns.
- **The signer** is a service on the control host that orch calls over a unix socket (`--signer-socket`), HTTP with JSON bodies. Orch makes three calls, which [the signer's document](./signer.md) fixes: `POST /v1/host` with `statement` and `csr`, answered with `certificate`; `GET /v1/crl`, answered with the signed list; `POST /v1/crl` with a signed list as the body, answered with the list the signer holds. A 401 or 403 is the signer's refusal; anything else that fails is a 502 to the caller. Orch does not ask for its own identity: the signer issues an orch certificate only for a request root signs, so root has the signer issue it (`POST /v1/orch`) and places it, with the key and the authority, at the paths orch is given; a member without them does not start. The revocation key's public half is a PKIX PEM, which `naru-signer init --revocation-public-key` writes.
- **The host's agent** in `naru-hostd` runs when `--orch-url` is set. It trusts orch by the SHA-256 of the authority's certificate (`--orch-ca-sha256`), which must be in the chain orch presents, and by orch's name, so a host's own certificate cannot stand in for orch. It reads the enrollment key from `--enroll-key-file`, keeps its key at `--host-key`, writes the certificate beside it and then heartbeats; with a valid certificate for the key it does not enroll again.
- **Drain** ([ADR 0042](./adr/0042-the-groups-engine-plans-from-observation-alone-and-replaces-a-stateful-member-only-after-its-fence-has-passed.md), note of 2026-11-14). The host record in etcd carries `draining` (false when absent). `POST /v1/hosts/{id}/drain` and `POST /v1/hosts/{id}/undrain` are for an operator, set and clear it, answer the host as `GET /v1/hosts` lists it (with `draining`), are 404 for a host orch does not hold, and each is an event, `host.drained` and `host.undrained`, whose detail names the caller. Setting the flag on a host that already has it is not an error. The flag changes no host: the groups engine reads it on its next pass.
- **Groups.**
  - The API stores a group spec under `/naru/orch/groups/<name>` and the engine's member records under `/naru/orch/group-members/<id>`, each written with a compare-and-swap on its modification revision. A spec is validated before it is stored (400); `GET` shows the members with their phase computed from the hosts, or the stored phase and `observed: false` when no host list could be had. `DELETE` is 409 while the group has a member that is not gone: apply it with `count: 0` first.
  - `GET /v1/groups` and `GET /v1/groups/{name}` carry `hold` (time and message) on a group with fewer live members than its count, from the newest `group.hold` event the log holds for it, only when that event is newer than twice the engine's repeat interval (an ongoing hold is re-recorded once per interval, so an older one has ended); the API reads the log only when a group is short. `naructl group get` prints it, `group list` marks the group in a HELD column, and `naructl events` lists the newest events (`--group` keeps one group's and its members').
  - **Boot sets** ([ADR 0089](./adr/0089-orch-adds-the-boot-set-a-group-needs-to-the-hosts-that-place-it-from-a-release-store-it-is-configured-with.md), accepted). With `--boot-set-store` (an `https://` URL or a directory laid out as a release's assets: `naru-node-<version>.json`, the kernel and initramfs it names, `SHA256SUMS` and `SHA256SUMS.sigstore.json`), the leader adds the boot set a group's `nodeVersion` needs to each admitted, ready, undrained host in the group's region and labels that lacks it, by streaming the release's kernel and initramfs from the store into the host's `POST /v1/boot-sets` with orch's host-client certificate, at most two hosts at once. `nodeVersion` is a release version, or a name the host already offers (`naru`, or a set added by hand), which is taken as it is and not fetched; from a release version orch derives the name `naru-node-` and the version in lower case with every character outside `a-z`, `0-9` and `-` made `-` (`v0.1.0-dev.5` is `naru-node-v0-1-0-dev-5`; a name over 63 characters is refused). Placement, the host call that creates the microVM and this reconciler resolve the name with one function (`groups.ResolveBootSet`). It never removes a boot set.
    - **Verification comes before any byte is sent.** `internal/groups/release` reads the store and checks, in this order: the cosign keyless bundle `SHA256SUMS.sigstore.json` over `SHA256SUMS` (sigstore-go v1.3.0) against the trusted root, the certificate identity and issuer of `--boot-set-identity` (a trailing `*` is a prefix, and a prefix must be at least `https://github.com/<owner>/<repo>/`; `{version}` in it is the version asked for; the flag defaults to `https://github.com/garamsh/naru/.github/workflows/release.yml@refs/tags/{version}`, the release workflow at the tag of that version, so a release signed for another tag does not verify) and `--boot-set-issuer` (`https://token.actions.githubusercontent.com` by default), with a signed certificate timestamp, a transparency-log entry and an inclusion proof required; then `naru-node-<version>.json` against its line of `SHA256SUMS`, its name and version against the version asked for, and the digests of the two files against the manifest's; then each file as it streams, by size and SHA-256, so that a file that differs ends the upload with an error before the host commits (the host checks the digests as well). The check needs no network beyond the store: a bundle without an inclusion proof is a failure, and a check is never skipped when the Sigstore services are unreachable. The trust root is `--boot-set-trust-root` (a `trusted_root.json`) or the public-good root built in, `internal/groups/release/trusted_root.json`, fetched with sigstore-go's TUF client on 2026-11-21 (SHA-256 `844a1c6de3986c9f02070266b25e0d1a2fa99ceccc89f6b9ad90aae47b62a16e`); a fleet that must follow a rotation of the public-good root gives the current file with the flag or takes an orch built with the new one. An identity set to empty, or a trust root or identity that cannot be built, is an error at start; without `--boot-set-store` the feature is off and `naructl host boot-set add` is the only way. The two files together may not exceed the host's 1 GiB less 1 MiB. An `https://` store is read with the system's roots only (`SSL_CERT_FILE` and `SSL_CERT_DIR` replace them), follows a redirect only to `https` on the host it was configured with (at most 5), and is given up on when it sends nothing for 30 s; the small assets (`SHA256SUMS`, the bundle, the manifest) are also bounded to 2 minutes. A `nodeVersion` that begins `naru-node-` is a boot set name and not a release version, and no release is fetched for it.
    - **Failures.** A release that does not verify, a store that cannot be read, or a host that refuses the set records `bootset.failed` on the host, naming the check (`signature check failed`, `signer identity check failed`, `checksum check failed`, `manifest check failed`, `release asset not found`, `release store unreadable`) at most once per 10 minutes for the same host, name and check (the wording of the detail does not make another), and the host is retried after one pass interval, then two, doubling to at most 10 minutes while it keeps failing, the wait starting again when the set is no longer lacking; a success records `bootset.added`. The group stays held with the node-version reason until a host offers the set. `init` does not yet write the four flags: `spec.orch` has no field for them.
  - The leader runs the engine over three adapters. The store is the etcd one. The events go to the inventory's event log as `group.<kind>`, a hold that repeats once per 10 minutes. The hosts are each admitted host's own API (`lib/hostapi`) at the address its enrollment recorded, over mutual TLS with the pair `--host-client-cert` and `--host-client-key` (orch's own pair by default, which init passes explicitly), trusting `--host-ca` (the authority by default; `spec.orch.hostCA` from init). The host takes that pair's OrganizationalUnit `orch` as the provisioner role ([ADR 0050](./adr/0050-the-image-carries-orch-and-the-signer-and-init-runs-them-as-separate-users-from-spec-orch-spec-signer-and-spec-metrics.md), note of 2026-11-14). `--group-interval` sets the pause between passes.
  - A host is ready while its `/v1/host` and `/v1/microvms` answer; its capacity is what `/v1/host` reports free, and the names of the boot sets it offers (`mvmd.bootSets` of `/v1/host`, the configured ones and the added ones, not its kernels) are its node versions. It is draining when its inventory record says so (see **Drain**).
  - A template maps to the host API as follows: the image form's `version` is the image (`repository@sha256:digest`) and its root disk is the template's `rootDiskBytes`, which must be above zero; the OS form's `nodeVersion` is the boot set, its first disk the data disk, and its seed the template's `userData`, or `#cloud-config` when that is empty; the template's `addresses` fix the members' addresses by number (member `<group>-<n>` has `addresses[n-1]`), and placement refuses a host none of whose Machine prefixes holds the member's address ([ADR 0099](./adr/0099-a-group-template-lists-its-members-addresses-by-number-orch-places-a-member-on-a-host-whose-machine-prefix-holds-its-address-and-mvmd-gives-it.md)). A template that names a `network` creates the microVM attached to it.
  - `GET /v1/microvms` reports each microVM's image digest or boot set and its network, as its host reports them.
  - A stateful member is created fenced and not started. Renewing sets the host's per-microVM fence to the deadline the engine chose, its length rounded down to whole seconds and its renewal number that deadline in Unix nanoseconds; inspecting reads the time the fence has left, counted from the clock at the call, and `fenceLapsedAt`.
- **Networks** ([ADR 0049](./adr/0049-orch-keeps-the-networks-and-allocates-each-members-pod-prefix-by-compare-and-swap-and-the-leader-tells-every-host-its-networks-members-and-peers.md)).
  - The API stores a network, `{prefix, apiEndpoint?}`, under `/naru/orch/networks/<name>`. The name is a DNS label, the prefix an IPv4 prefix with its host bits clear, the endpoint an address inside it (400). `DELETE`, or an apply that changes the prefix, is 409 while a group's template names the network. Each apply and delete is an event, `network.applied` and `network.deleted`.
  - The leader's network reconciler runs one pass per `--network-interval` (default 10 s). For every admitted host that answers `GET /v1/tunnel` and `GET /v1/microvms` it declares every network on it with `PUT /v1/networks/{name}`, whose `remoteMembers` are the addresses (`/32`) of the network's microVMs on the other hosts, and puts the whole list of peers with `PUT /v1/peers`: each other host that answered, its tunnel's key, its endpoint at the host's address with the tunnel's port, its Machine prefixes and its mesh address, with the host's own mesh address beside them. It then lists the host's networks with `GET /v1/networks` and deletes with `DELETE /v1/networks/{name}` each that orch does not keep, so a network deleted on orch leaves every host. A host that does not answer is logged and skipped for the pass, but its peers are kept on the other hosts until 3 consecutive probes have failed (K, [groups.md](./groups.md) §Bounds on what a pass asks of the world). Nothing else is kept between passes, so a host that restarted, or orch after a restore, is brought whole again within one interval.
- **naructl** takes `orch init|backup|restore`, `host list|pending|admit|label`, `orch ca` (the orch authority, read from the signer, since orch serves none), `enroll-key create|revoke`, `group apply -f|get|list|delete`, `events [--group NAME] [--limit N]` and `network apply -f|get|list|delete` ([config.md](./config.md)).
- **Deployment.** orchd runs from `spec.orch` as user 6002, from the binary in the `naru-orch` system extension, which also holds the signer: a squashfs image the Node stores on its data disk, named by its SHA-256 in `spec.extensions`, and init mounts read-only at `/usr/lib/naru/extensions/naru-orch` before any service ([ADR 0055](./adr/0055-orch-and-the-signer-are-delivered-as-a-system-extension-a-squashfs-image-init-mounts-read-only.md)). Upgrading a member means delivering the new release's image and changing the digest with the bundle.

- **Admitting a host that booted from an install seed** ([ADR 0059](./adr/0059-orch-admits-a-host-that-boots-from-an-install-seed-allocates-its-machine-prefix-and-builds-its-configuration-from-one-fleet-profile.md)).
  - A host's enrollment may carry its hardware (processors, memory, disks with their size, model and partition count, links with carrier and MAC), which `GET /v1/pending` returns beside the pairing code. A host that reports hardware is a seed-booted host.
  - The fleet's host profile is one etcd key, `/naru/orch/profile`: `PUT /v1/profile` (operator and root) replaces it, `GET /v1/profile` (reader, automation, operator and root) returns it with every secret value (a registry password) replaced by `<set>` and the rest of its shape kept, so no role reads a secret back. `PUT /v1/profile` with `<set>` where a password belongs is 422, and the stored profile is kept: orch has no rule that keeps a stored secret for a redacted one, so the operator writes the password again. Admission of a host with hardware and no profile is 409.
  - At admission orch allocates the host's Machine prefix, the first free /24 of the profile's pool (`orch/internal/prefixes`), skipping the first /24 when the pool has more than one; a full pool is 409. Admission is reserve-then-commit: orch first writes the allocation `/naru/orch/allocations/<prefix>` as a reservation that lapses after 2 minutes, in a transaction that fails if any allocation was written between the read and the write, so two admissions never hold overlapping prefixes; only then does it ask the signer for the certificate, and it commits the allocation, the host and the removal of the pending record in one transaction. A signer failure releases the reservation, so a full pool is 409 before any certificate is issued. A host without hardware keeps the order it had. Before it reserves a prefix, the admission of a host with hardware is 409 while an admitted host has no confirmed allocation, naming the host ([ADR 0103](./adr/0103-orch-records-the-machine-prefixes-every-host-reports-and-admits-a-joined-host-only-while-every-admitted-host-has-one-recorded.md)). The configuration is built by `lib/layout`'s `HostOnly` mode from the profile, the host ID and the prefix, and is handed to the host in the answer to its poll once it is admitted. The configuration is not stored: orch builds it from the host's record and the profile as it is at each answer, so the registry password is in etcd only in the profile. A profile that is missing at that time answers 409 and one that no longer checks answers 409. A start of a new leader rewrites host records that an older orch wrote with a `config` field, without it; the rewrite is idempotent.
  - The built configuration has `metadata.name` the host ID, a `NARUDATA` data disk at `/var/lib/naru`, `spec.api.keyFile` and the host's certificate, `spec.host.orch.enrollKey: joined`, and no `spec.signer`, `spec.orch` or `spec.join`. A host whose certificate has expired enrolls again only from a new seed. A revoked or expired enrollment key stays 403.
  - **Every host has a mesh address** ([ADR 0113](./adr/0113-hosts-form-a-wireguard-mesh-with-an-address-each-hosts-that-cannot-be-reached-dial-out-to-relays-and-orch-rotates-every-hosts-key-amending-adr-0045-item-5-adr-0048-item-5-and-adr-0049-item-6.md) items 1 and 2). The profile's optional `meshPool` is an IPv4 prefix without host bits, not the default route, that overlaps neither the Machine pool nor the management prefix; orch also refuses it with 409 when it overlaps a network or an address pool, and a network (409) or an address pool (400) that overlaps it, all under the address-space guard, so the check and the write are one transaction. At admission, when the profile has a pool, orch reserves the host's mesh address beside its Machine prefix, by the same reserve-then-commit, under `/naru/orch/mesh-addresses/<address>`: the first free /32, skipping the pool's first address when the pool is wider than /31; a full pool is 409 before any certificate is issued, and a reservation is never committed from a profile that changed since the address was picked. This holds for a host enrolled with its own configuration too. The recorder gives every admitted host without one an address on each pass, so hosts admitted before the pool, or while the profile had none, get theirs. `GET /v1/hosts` shows it as `meshAddress`, the network reconciler sends it to the host, and removing the host frees it. A profile whose pool would leave outside it an address a host holds, or that drops the pool while any host holds one, is 409.
  - **Every host's Machine prefix is recorded** ([ADR 0103](./adr/0103-orch-records-the-machine-prefixes-every-host-reports-and-admits-a-joined-host-only-while-every-admitted-host-has-one-recorded.md)). The leader's recorder reads `mvmd.machinePrefixes` of `GET /v1/host` from every admitted host once per `--network-interval` and writes each prefix as a confirmed allocation of that host, so a host enrolled with its own configuration (its `machinePrefix` stays empty) is as known to the pool as one that joined from a seed. A prefix another host holds stays with the first and is the event `host.prefix-conflict`, and a pass releases the confirmed allocations of hosts that are no longer admitted.
  - `DELETE /v1/hosts/{id}` (operator and root) removes a host in one transaction that also frees every allocation of the host, and appends `host.removed`. It does not revoke the host's certificate: `naructl operator revoke` does.

## Open questions

- **Exposures (orch and host built).** The operator declares an exposure: a LAN address from an address pool that forwards ports to members of one network ([ADR 0085](./adr/0085-a-service-of-type-loadbalancer-is-fulfilled-by-a-naru-cloud-controller-manager-that-asks-orch-for-an-address-from-a-pool-one-host-announces-it-on-the-lan-and-forwards-it-to-the-nodes-nodeports.md) for the pools, the leases and the hosts' side; [ADR 0102](./adr/0102-the-operator-declares-an-exposure-of-a-networks-members-at-any-backend-port-so-the-leased-lan-address-reaches-a-members-api-server-and-an-owner-table-holds-the-exposer-to-the-nodeport-range-amending-adr-0085-items-4-6-and-7.md) for the operator's exposure). Orch keeps the pools and exposures in etcd (`orch/internal/exposures`), claims an address in the transaction that writes its exposure, and the leader's reconciler leases each address to one host by rendezvous hashing, renewing it every 5 s for 15 s and moving it only after the old host confirmed the removal or the lease and a 2 s skew passed. The operator applies one with `PUT /v1/networks/{name}/exposures/{id}`, reads one with `GET` of the same path and releases one with `DELETE` of it; `GET /v1/exposures` lists every network's for a reader. An exposure is stored under its network and id (`/naru/orch/exposures/<network>/<id>`), so an id is the network's own: another network's exposure of the same id is not seen (404, the answer for an absent one). A spec that names another network than the path is 403, as is a pool that does not list the network. A create is one transaction that holds only while the address is unclaimed, the pool is still the one the plan used and the network's count (`/naru/orch/exposure-counts/<network>`) is the one read, so the 64-exposure cap holds under concurrent creates; a pool is replaced or deleted only while the claims are the ones the check saw. A delete does not free the address: the record is marked `releasing` (a get no longer shows it; the list does), and the leader's reconciler deletes it, and so frees the address, when the host that held it answered that it removed it or the lease deadline and 2 s have passed. The reconciler treats an exposure whose network is gone the same way, so deleting a network cascades to its exposures within a pass. Each pass stores the lease deadline before it asks the host, as the call's allowance (2 s) plus the lease, so the deadline is never earlier than the end of the host's lease however late the renewal arrived; every call of a pass is bounded by that allowance, and the hosts are asked at once, so one host that does not answer delays no other. A microVM counts as a backend, and as a reason for a host to be a candidate, only while it runs. A host is a candidate for an exposure only if it runs at least one of the exposure's backends on the exposure's network (ADR 0085 item 6, as amended by [#296](https://github.com/garamsh/naru/pull/296)); the lease sent to a host lists only the backends that run on that network on that host, so the host forwards only to live local microVMs. The reconciler stores those backends with the lease deadline; a silent holder (members unknown) is renewed with that last-known list so it keeps receiving exactly the list it already accepted. The network scope prevents a backend address shared across networks from matching a microVM on the wrong network. Renewal re-evaluates both the candidate set and the per-host backend list each pass. A network prefix that overlaps an address pool is refused, as a pool that overlaps a prefix or another pool is: each write runs its check after reading a guard key (`/naru/orch/address-space`) and commits only while the guard is unchanged, moving it, so two writes made from the same reading are not both kept (the later gets 409). A `releasing` record still holds its address and counts against the network's 64-exposure cap until the reconciler deletes it. A host that is admitted but did not answer the listing keeps the addresses it holds, renewed as before, for 30 s; past that it is taken for gone and they move once the lease and skew pass, so a slow holder does not lose an address and take it back. An apply over a record the reconciler has just renewed is read again, up to three times, before it answers 409. **Ports and owner:** an exposure's port is `{protocol, port, backendPort}`, its protocol `TCP` or `UDP`, the one spelling orch, the host API and hostd share ([ADR 0107](./adr/0107-an-exposed-ports-protocol-is-one-type-spelled-tcp-or-udp-in-lib-wire-that-orchs-api-the-host-api-and-hostd-share-amending-adr-0085-items-4-and-6.md)), and its port and backend port are 1 to 65535 (400 otherwise). Every exposure records its `owner`, `operator` for each one orch makes ([ADR 0114](./adr/0114-naru-runs-no-kubernetes-or-gaon-and-their-layouts-programs-images-tests-and-documents-are-removed.md)). An exposure an exposer stored before ADR 0114, and one stored before ADR 0102 with no owner, is not leased: the reconciler logs it at error level on each pass and records an `exposure.refused` event once. The operator reads and deletes it, and an apply over its id is 409 until the operator has deleted it.

- `naructl orch init` writes the recovery key and its public half only. The root person file and the first control host's identities come from the signer's own commands, which this change does not have.
- The rate limits are per member, so an attacker who spreads requests over three members is allowed three times the rate.
- A fence's deadline as mvmd keeps it is the moment it receives the renewal plus the length, so it can be later than the recorded one by the call's latency; the engine's wait for a lapsed host is not extended for it.
- A group template carries no root disk size, no user-data and no host-API role. The root disk and the seed are fixed as above, and the host API still reads the role `naru:host-provisioner` from the certificate, not `lib/identity`'s roles, so orch's pair for the hosts carried that Organization until the host API took `lib/identity`'s orch role (see the ADR 0050 note).
- A host that stops answering is not ready after one failed probe; there is no grace period before the engine replaces its members.
- A joined host's configuration carries the sentinel enrollment key `joined`: orch holds only the hash of the key the host joined with, and a joined host renews nothing. A new seed is needed once its one-year certificate lapses, and host certificate renewal is not decided ([ADR 0059](./adr/0059-orch-admits-a-host-that-boots-from-an-install-seed-allocates-its-machine-prefix-and-builds-its-configuration-from-one-fleet-profile.md)).
- Machine-prefix allocations are keys per prefix, not per host, with the host recorded in the value: etcd's create-if-absent on the prefix is what keeps two admissions from taking one prefix, and a per-host key could not.
- **Machines (the API built, the rest decided, not built).** Beside the Machine API and its records (§The Machine API), orch keeps each Machine's binding, move record and routes in etcd, and runs the Machine reconciler `orch/internal/machines` on the leader; the operator API gains `POST /v1/machines/{caller}/{name}/move`, a drain moves a host's Machines, and `POST /v1/hosts/{id}/abandon`, given the operator's attestation that the host is fenced (powered off), records the abandonment and the attestation, revokes the host and lets its Machines be rebound. [machines.md](./machines.md) has the whole; [ADR 0108](./adr/0108-orch-serves-a-versioned-machine-api-that-names-machines-and-never-hosts-to-callers-holding-a-signer-issued-caller-certificate.md), [ADR 0109](./adr/0109-each-host-is-authoritative-for-what-runs-on-it-and-keeps-running-it-without-orch-orch-keeps-desired-state-placement-and-routes-and-a-machine-is-rebound-only-on-its-old-hosts-acknowledgement-or-abandonment.md) and [ADR 0110](./adr/0110-orch-places-a-machine-on-the-host-that-fits-its-shape-by-capacity-orch-accounts-for-itself-and-labels-and-spreads-machines-across-hosts-amending-adr-0042s-placement.md) the decisions.
- **Routes, aliases, relays and key rotation (decided, not built).** Orch declares relays to unreachable hosts, rotates each host's tunnel key past `--mesh-key-max-age` (30 days), and reaches a host outside the site at its mesh address ([ADR 0113](./adr/0113-hosts-form-a-wireguard-mesh-with-an-address-each-hosts-that-cannot-be-reached-dial-out-to-relays-and-orch-rotates-every-hosts-key-amending-adr-0045-item-5-adr-0048-item-5-and-adr-0049-item-6.md)). It keeps the aliases (`PUT /v1/callers/{name}` grants a caller's `aliasDomains`), derives the routing table, pushes it whole to every proxy host and records each one's acknowledged revision, deletes a microVM whose address a route named only after every proxy has acknowledged a table without it (or `--route-max-age` has passed), and leases proxy exposures (`PUT /v1/proxy-exposures/{id}`) to hosts whose proxy answers ([ADR 0112](./adr/0112-a-machine-is-reached-by-a-stable-name-through-naru-proxy-which-terminates-tls-reads-the-routes-orch-pushes-to-its-host-and-forwards-over-the-mesh-amending-adr-0085-items-3-5-and-6.md), [proxy.md](./proxy.md)).
